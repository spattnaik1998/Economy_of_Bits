## What notebook 04 established

**The tail buys capital while everyone else gets lighter.**

| Group | asset lightness 2016 → 2025 | slope/yr | *p* | capex/revenue 2016 → 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **tail** (ever top 1%) | 0.845 → **0.647** | −0.0190 | <0.0001 | 0.088 → **0.168** |
| digital, non-tail | 0.869 → 0.893 | +0.0022 | <0.0001 | 0.068 → 0.102 |
| rest | 0.718 → 0.747 | +0.0040 | 0.0001 | 0.105 → 0.116 |

Gross margin over the same years is flat in every group (tail *p* = 0.21). It is capital,
not margins. Firm by firm: Meta 0.868 → 0.518, Microsoft 0.905 → 0.669, Alphabet 0.796 →
0.586, Amazon 0.651 → 0.564 — while **Nvidia goes the other way**, 0.937 → 0.944, and
Broadcom 0.952 → 0.985. Nvidia is fabless and the fabs belong to TSMC. Compute sits on the
balance sheet of whoever runs it.

**H1 is rejected, in the opposite direction.** The Gabaix–Ibragimov rank regression gives
high-bit-intensity firms a *thinner* tail: +0.211 at *p* = 0.0015. The membership tests are
monotone and clean — bit intensity predicts the top 25% (*p* < 0.0001), 10% (*p* = 0.003)
and 5% (*p* = 0.024), and is flat at the top 1% (*p* = 0.96).

**Gibrat survives.** Growth on size +0.007, *p* = 0.12, with no bit-intensity or platform
interaction. So the tail is not being generated by increasing returns to size.

**The tail churns moderately.** Ten firms passed through a five-slot top 1%; only Apple and
Microsoft are there in all ten years. Shorrocks mobility 0.50.

## The four defects

1. **ExxonMobil is mapped to CIK 2115436** — a 2025 re-registration with one year of
   filings. Its real history is CIK 34088. BlackRock has the same problem. XOM enters the
   tail group in 2025 *alone*, at an asset lightness of 0.333, and pulls down the 2025 group
   mean — inflating the very slope being reported.
2. **Alphabet is split across GOOG and GOOGL**, so it appears as two firms with broken
   series. Firm identity has to key on CIK.
3. **ASC 842.** From 2019 Amazon, Meta and Alphabet report PP&E under a tag that folds in
   finance-lease right-of-use assets, switching in and out of it mid-series. 321 firm-years
   are on it, rising from 7 in 2019 to 69 in 2025.
4. **Berkshire has no PP&E figure in ten years.**

## What notebook 05 does

The fix for ASC 842 is a different measure rather than a better one. **Capital expenditure
over revenue is a cash-flow quantity that lease capitalisation cannot touch**, and it
measures the build-out directly. It becomes the headline; asset lightness stays as
corroboration with three explicit tests.

Also: CIK overrides with a detection rule that runs before them, firm identity on CIK,
every trend computed twice — all firms and a balanced ten-year panel — and hyperscalers
separated from the rest of the tail, which is the sharpest test of the mechanism.

## Two answers already visible in the dry run

**Leases are not the explanation.** Splitting the tail's asset-lightness trend at 2019: the
pre-2019 slope is already −0.023 (*p* = 0.0035), the step at 2019 is +0.019 and *not*
significant (*p* = 0.20), and the change in slope after is *p* = 0.39. The decline was
underway before ASC 842 and shows no lease step.

**The H1 inversion is not pharmaceuticals.** Ranking firms only against their own sector
makes it *stronger*, not weaker: +0.321 at *p* < 0.0001 against +0.211 raw. High-bit-intensity
firms have thinner tails within their own industries. That is a real finding and it points
the opposite way from chapter 6.

One nuance the balanced panel adds: capex intensity rises steeply for digital non-tail firms
too (0.068 → 0.154, *p* < 0.0001), not only the tail (0.088 → 0.204). The capital turn is
broad across the digital economy, sharpest at the top. The paper should say that rather than
claiming it is confined to the giants.

## Where this leaves the argument

Concentration rose steeply and is robust. It is **not** explained by the economics of bits:
firms whose output is most bit-like sit in the upper-middle of the distribution, and within
their own sectors they have *thinner* tails. Growth remains Gibrat-like, so the tail is not
produced by increasing returns to size. And the firms that occupy the tail have spent the
decade acquiring exactly the rival, physical capital those economics were defined against.

That is a negative result with a mechanism, and it lands squarely on the JRFM Future Scope
prediction — the binding constraint moving from software to compute — which makes the
companion paper a continuation rather than a repetition.
